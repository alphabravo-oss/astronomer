Registration duration projects the writer's `success` and `failed` outcomes, with `other` for unknown inputs. Vault resolves preserve `succeeded` and `failed`, with `other` for unknown inputs. Agent and tunnel state metrics share a fixed projection of the maintained informer families; custom or future values use `other`. The original state payload, rate limiter key and SSE kind remain unchanged. Discovered Gatekeeper resource types are already published as the literal `Constraint` by the agent.

Registration phases retain one current phase child per observed cluster, including `other` for a future unknown phase. A successful write captures its metric owner and sequence inside the transaction; publication is buffered until commit. A retired owner or out-of-order late flush cannot recreate a child. Capture does not publish a phase, so discarded transactions export no phase change. Such logical captures remain bounded and can be retired by a later complete absent snapshot.

The owner map has the same engineering ceiling of 10,000 as the connection observation. This is not a measured fleet-size claim. Overflow drops only metrics, with finite `budget_dropped` admission telemetry; it never rejects a registration write. A flush whose captured owner was retired, or superseded by a later committed publication, records `late_dropped`. This includes a newly created row that commits after a complete older database observation retired its unpublished capture: the product commit remains valid, while that phase observation is dropped until the next complete phase resynchronization; no additional transition is required. The `captured` count is a capture count, not committed mutations or durable metric delivery.

Only the successful, uncanceled, bounded read-only repeatable-read cluster snapshot invokes registration retirement. Partial/error/canceled/over-budget observations preserve prior phase children. Every admitted capture renews its watermark, including existing owners, so an earlier in-flight observation cannot retire a newly admitted transition. Current owners retain their phase child until a new committed transition replaces it; no global Reset is used. Remaining Vault/name lifetimes, other metric owner families, real PostgreSQL snapshot execution, scale and collector qualification are still open.

The follow-up authoritative phase collector closes the missing-terminal-phase gap: it periodically resynchronizes committed phases from canonical ListClusters within its own bounded read-only repeatable-read transaction. A complete later snapshot restores a committed `ready` phase even when the original captured flush was retired while that row was uncommitted. Snapshot publication has a unique monotonic read watermark, checks the final capped registry before any changes, retires before adding, and refuses a stale result after a newer complete absence observation. Captured callbacks retain pointer/sequence identity and cannot alter a recreated owner's phase. An admission after the read watermark remains protected from an earlier phase observation.

The phase loop is supervised, serial, and ticks every 15 seconds. Its ten-second transaction reads at most 21 pages/10,000 rows, then checks cancellation after commit; every failure preserves prior metrics and completion time. No gauge is published from an uncommitted capture. Snapshot gauges describe committed state at the recorded observation time, not immediate cross-process deletion visibility. Worker tombstone/delete happens in a separate process; the next authoritative server observation confirms absence. No early retirement occurs merely because the HTTP endpoint queued decommission work. The additional database queries and extra phase resync workload have not yet been benchmarked.

Phase/Vault owner observations export finite complete/budget/invalid/canceled/failed results (phase also has stale), with last-complete collection-start timestamps. Admission counters distinguish capture/budget/late drops; those do not imply committed mutation counts or durable delivery. Unrecognized diagnostic text maps to the fixed failed result.
